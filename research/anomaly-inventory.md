# Unusual entries and corrected findings

The original Mac and a fresh signed-out installation on the same physical hardware provide two different kinds of comparison. An entry can be rare among rows in one database, present on both installations, or different between them. Neither installation supplies a population estimate for other Macs. The [README](../README.md) gives the exact APNs and VFS counts and the conclusion on developer attribution.

## Findings that changed on follow-up

- **Which executable owns the keychain identity?** The Nov 13 comparison checked the executable named `securityd`. Later work identified the signed owner of the cached `com.apple.securityd` identity as `secd`. All 19 cached key/value pairs of the base identity matched signed `secd` in the two earlier subject captures. The extra string `"true"` in the shared-keychain variant has a shipped compatibility-code explanation. The original signature mismatch and the outstanding historical-writer question are distinct. [S4, S6, S11, S20]
- **Was the guest a signed-in shared-keychain control?** The four signed-out checkpoints had zero exact `kcsharing` identity rows. Two later checks after reported sign-in each still had 43 app rows, zero exact target rows, and the same 43 identity/entitlement pairs as signed-out shutdown. Settings showed the sign-in form. The Nov 20 follow-up also documented HTTPS timeouts for four Apple endpoints and an independent control in the guest while the host reached all five. The purported authenticated and shared-group checkpoints remained unverified. [S12, S25]
- **Was there a non-Apple route or a local CloudKit server?** An early JSON-only extraction missed endpoints. Across the decoded configuration and the cached container URL fields, the Nov 19 audit counted 279 HTTPS occurrences and five hostnames. A narrow suffix filter had also flagged `media.icloud-content.com`, whose parent domain family appears in Apple documentation. The configured China-named endpoint had one occurrence. The audit could establish neither use of that route nor a local CloudKit listener from these fields. Its socket visibility was incomplete. [S1, S3, S10]
- **Do development labels identify a developer’s action?** The original `Development` iCloud labels were confirmed in signed `homeeventsd` and `businessservicesd`. The Nov 19 scoped enrollment output reported no automated enrollment or MDM, and the user-scoped profile listing returned none. Local signing and enrollment describe different things. Neither identifies who changed a personal account or deployed a schema. [S1, S8, S20] [Developer-attribution evidence](../README.md#did-someone-with-developer-privileges-change-this-system)

## How the counts were checked

The Nov 21 recount used read-only, immutable connections to the three **retained logical backups**. The README’s denominators count every `AppBundle` row in each backup. For the numerator, it checks the exact text at the top-level `com.apple.developer.aps-environment` key and JSON Boolean true at the exact `com.apple.private.vfs.open-by-id` key. The VFS owner split groups matching logical rows by their base application identity. The association-option comparison counts each row in `AppContainerAccount` once per named top-level Boolean set to true in `containerOptions`. The environment comparison counts only the `environment` and `apsEnvironment` labels from the two corresponding tables.

Guest counts came from the four retained typed-count exports. For this page’s service-name table we used the comparative report’s named-term row measure (`prior_terminal_rule`). An exact service-map pair, a Boolean entitlement, and a row containing a literal answer different questions. For example, the guest had two rows referring to `DigitalHealth` at desktop and one exact encrypted-remote-management-to-`DigitalHealth` map. [S11, S12, S26]
